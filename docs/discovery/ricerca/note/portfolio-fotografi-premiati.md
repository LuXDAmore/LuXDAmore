# Award-winning individual photographer portfolio sites (2022-2026, plus older classics flagged)

Method note: award data comes from each site's Awwwards page (I downloaded the pages and read the award date, credited author, tags, "featured elements" and palette). I checked whether each site is live and pulled og:image with an HTTP request to the live homepage on 2026-11-24. **Stack hints** marked "(HTML)" come from string matches in the served homepage HTML, such as `/_nuxt/`, `__NUXT__`, `/_next/`, `webflow`, `wp-content`, `sanity`, `storyblok`, `/_astro/`, `barba`, `lenis`, `swup`. They are evidence, not proof, and JS bundles were not inspected. Awwwards "palette" values are Awwwards' coarse colour-filter buckets (for example #404040 = "dark grey" bucket), not exact brand colours, unless a page gives exact hex. No site was rendered in a browser, so the descriptions of interactions come from Awwwards element titles and descriptions and from Codrops/Commarts write-ups.

## Which photographer portfolio sites have won Awwwards/FWA/CSSDA awards or been featured on Godly/Siteinspire/Hoverstat.es? (URL, photographer, studio, award, date)

### Takeaway
Awwwards is by far the richest source. Between 2023 and 2026 it gave Site of the Day (SOTD) to at least 7 individual photographer or cinematographer portfolios, and Honorable Mention (HM) to 15 or more. The most decorated recent builders are The Blackpepper Studio (Mark Clennon, O'shane Howard, Dondre Green, Meech213), Garden Eight (Takamitsu Motoyoshi, Kenichi Aikawa) and Exo Ape (Ottografie 2018 and 2025). Recent FWA, CSSDA, Godly and Hoverstat.es data for individual photographers was thin or unverifiable.

### Inferences
- Award density is high in 2025-2026 HMs, so "Awwwards HM" is a realistic bar. SOTD for an individual photographer site still needs a distinctive concept, such as Clennon's globe "Diary", Motoyoshi's speed-reactive infinite scroll, or Brimhall's film-exposure metaphors.
- Studios behind multiple photographer winners can serve as benchmarks: Blackpepper (4 sites), Garden Eight (2), Exo Ape (2 versions of Ottografie).
- Photographer sites age fast. Of four pre-2024 SOTD photographer sites I checked, two domains are dead (Mercure, Izbash) and one has been rebuilt on Cargo (Paul & Henriette). Keep this in mind when using older classics as references.

### Gaps
- **Godly.website / Hoverstat.es / CSSDA:** search returned no indexed photographer entries, and I did not browse those galleries directly. That coverage is missing.
- **FWA:** I found no verified 2022-2026 photographer FOTDs beyond the unconfirmed Carter Ogunsola case.
- **Motoyoshi award date:** the SOTD and Developer Award date (19 Feb 2025) comes from a search summary, not a direct read of the Awwwards page.
- **Bruno Arizio:** I did not verify his award status.
- **Landscape/travel photographers:** none of the 2025-2026 SOTD winners is clearly a landscape or travel photographer. Recognised sites skew toward fashion, beauty, portrait and cinematography. The closest matches to the client's genre are Jon McCormack (fine-art nature), Félix Péault (world in motion), Christie Hemm Klok (place-based stories), PaulKram (fine art) and Motoyoshi.

## What does each do that is special (navigation, image transitions, typography, color, pacing, negative space)?

### Takeaway
The recurring award-winning devices are:
- infinite or velocity-reactive scroll (Motoyoshi, Péault, Dondre Green, Samuel Regan)
- switchable grid/gallery or "contact sheet" views (Dondre Green, Brimhall, Ethan W)
- horizontal galleries with a fullscreen viewer (McCormack, GETZ)
- WebGL heroes with curtain or "morph" page transitions (Pothier, Brimhall, Per Appelgren)
- photography-native metaphors: film exposure, burn-through loader, contact sheet, "through the lens"

### Inferences
Several devices map directly onto the client's material:
- **Organising by series or collection** (PaulKram's "curated collections", Péault's "series", Tanya Timal's "one shooting scroll") fits his Lightroom grouping by colour and concept, and his "exhibition not shop" goal.
- **Velocity-reactive infinite scroll** (Motoyoshi) and **WebGL "throttle" galleries** (Brimhall) are natural vehicles for his "Blurred" and intentional motion-blur work. Scroll speed could drive a blur or smear shader.
- **Photography-native metaphors** (Brimhall's exposure/burn-through loader, Ethan W's contact-sheet menu) were explicitly called out as featured elements. Juries appear to reward UI ideas that come from the photographic process.
- **Globe/map views** (Clennon's "Diary (Globe View)", Sergio Musel's "Interactive Globe") are a proven pattern for travel archives.
  - Musel: HM 5 Dec 2025, Framer, UX designer and photographer, so a hybrid. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/sergio-musel)

### Gaps
- Element titles say *what* exists, not exactly how it looks or moves. Shader type, easing, timing and type families were not verified, because no site was rendered and no fonts were inspected.
- I found no reliable typography data (font names) for any site. Fontsinuse has a Dondre Green identity entry that I did not read. — [Fontsinuse](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/74047/dondre-green-identity-and-website)

### Inferences
- No recognised photographer site in this set is explicitly organised **by dominant colour**. Colour appears only as the site's accent (Mercure, O'shane Howard) or in non-photographer experiments (RBYB Again). A Blue/Green/Red/Orange "exhibition rooms" model, where the UI tint or background adapts per colour album, looks like open territory. This inference comes from absence in the sample, not an exhaustive check.
- Dark near-black palettes like Pothier's #0b0a0a suit chiaroscuro and heavy-black photography. White editorial layouts like McCormack's frame nature work as fine art. A dual mode (Aikawa's "Mode Change") could let both coexist.
